# Legal Position Note: DRE Electronic Record, Ballot PDF, and Paper Audit Evidence

## Purpose and status

This note frames the legal question for review by COMELEC Legal and the
appropriate technical and policy offices. It is a research and design note,
not a legal opinion, a request for a particular legal conclusion, or evidence
that COMELEC has approved this system.

The proposed system terminology is:

> **The ballot PDF is the canonical electronic DRE voting record and tally
> input.**

“Canonical electronic record” describes the system's data model and processing
rule. It does not, by naming alone, make the PDF the statutory “official
ballot,” waive a voter-verified paper audit trail (VVPAT), or determine which
record controls in a legal contest. Those consequences must be reconciled with
the governing election law, applicable COMELEC resolutions and instructions,
the approved system profile, and any procurement requirements.

## Proposed system rule

For the OpScan with DRE profile, the system proposes that:

1. A voter makes and confirms selections using the approved electronic ballot
   interface.
2. The appliance finalizes and durably accepts a complete, authenticated
   ballot PDF and its associated evidence. Durable acceptance is the system's
   cast event.
3. The accepted ballot PDF, validated against its signed canonical selection
   data and approved election context, is the input to the deterministic
   electronic tally.
4. A printed artifact, if required or selected, is separately identified by
   its approved function (for example, voter-verification paper, audit
   evidence, or a legally designated ballot). It is not silently treated as
   either controlling or supplementary by software.
5. Any discrepancy among voter-confirmed selections, the accepted PDF,
   machine-readable payload, and printed evidence is preserved and handled
   under an approved procedure; the system does not silently choose a winner
   among conflicting representations.

This is a proposed DRE record-and-tally architecture. It is not the system's
own determination of the legal status of its artifacts.

## Statutory and regulatory framework

### Republic Act No. 9369, amending RA 8436

RA 9369 makes several provisions relevant to this design:

- **Section 2(3)** defines “official ballot” in an AES as a **paper ballot**,
  printed or generated by the technology, that faithfully captures or
  represents votes recorded or to be recorded electronically.
- **Section 2(8)** separately defines a direct-recording electronic election
  system (DRE) as using electronic ballots and recording voting data and ballot
  images.
- **Section 5** authorizes COMELEC to use paper-based or DRE systems as it
  deems appropriate and practical. That authorization does not itself specify
  that a particular PDF artifact is the controlling record for a particular
  procurement or election.
- **Section 6(e), (f), and (n)** requires, among the minimum system
  capabilities, provision for a VVPAT, system auditability, and a voter
  verification mechanism.
- **Section 15** gives COMELEC authority to prescribe the electronic display
  and/or size and form of the official ballot, including requirements for
  review and changing selections before casting.
- **Sections 21 and 22** address counting under the automated system and
  election returns, including electronically stored results, printed returns,
  signatures, and public reading/announcement requirements.

Read together, these provisions recognize electronic voting data and DRE while
also expressly defining the official ballot as paper and requiring a paper
audit-trail capability. The statute therefore does not support the simple
claim that “a PDF is the official ballot because it is electronic.” Nor does
the paper definition, by itself, answer every operational question about
which record is used as the DRE machine's initial tally input. The approved
system rules must specify how the electronic result and required paper
verification evidence relate, how discrepancies are handled, and what record
controls for each purpose.

### Republic Act No. 8792, Electronic Commerce Act

RA 8792 gives electronic data messages, documents, and signatures legal
recognition subject to integrity, reliability, authentication, and other
requirements. Sections 6–11 address legal recognition, integrity/original
form, electronic signatures, retention, and authentication; Section 27
addresses government use and requires appropriate government rules or
guidelines for the relevant use.

The Act supports the proposition that an electronic PDF or another electronic
file is not denied legal effect merely because it is electronic. It does not
by itself reclassify that file as the election-law “official ballot,” override
election-specific safeguards, or settle which DRE artifact supplies the
official tally. Section 7 expressly preserves existing-law formalities for
validity. A cryptographic signature and hash can help prove integrity and
origin; they do not independently confer election-law status.

### Supreme Court interpretation of VVPAT

In *Bagumbayan-VNP Movement, Inc. v. COMELEC*, G.R. No. 222731, March 8, 2016,
the Supreme Court treated the statutory VVPAT capability as mandatory and
explained that voter verification must be paper-based. The ruling emphasizes
the voter's opportunity to verify that the machine recorded the intended
choice and the evidentiary role of the VVPAT in audit and protest contexts.
It is a substantial reason not to describe optional printing as satisfying
the VVPAT requirement without COMELEC's determination that the proposed
mechanism meets the governing rule.

### COMELEC Resolution No. 11089 (2024), 2025 RMA

Resolution No. 11089, Section 17, says that when COMELEC uses DRE voting
machines instead of paper-based OMR counting machines, VVPAT receipts are the
material equivalent of ballots **for purposes of that Resolution**. Sections
19(c) and 20 prescribe reading and counting VVPAT receipts during the RMA.
This is a concrete example of COMELEC assigning a defined role to DRE paper
receipts in a specific audit procedure. Its express limitation to that
Resolution means it should not be presented as a general designation of the
receipt as the primary election-day tally record or as a blanket answer for
other elections, system designs, or legal proceedings.

## What the authorities establish—and what remains open

| Proposition | Position for this proposal |
| --- | --- |
| DRE may record electronic voting data and ballot images | Recognized by RA 9369's DRE definition, subject to the AES framework and COMELEC implementation. |
| Electronic files and signatures may have legal effect | Recognized by RA 8792 when statutory requirements are met; this does not decide election-specific artifact status. |
| “Official ballot” under RA 9369 is paper | The statutory definition says paper, whether printed or generated by the technology, faithfully capturing or representing votes recorded or to be recorded electronically. |
| AES must provide VVPAT capability | Required by RA 9369 §6 and addressed by the Supreme Court in *Bagumbayan-VNP*. Whether a proposed PDF-plus-print workflow satisfies it needs a specific legal/technical determination. |
| DRE VVPAT receipt can be treated as a ballot in RMA | Resolution 11089 §17 does so for purposes of that RMA resolution; do not generalize beyond its scope. |
| This system's PDF can be its canonical electronic record and tally input | Yes as a proposed technical/system rule. Its use as the legally operative DRE tally input in an official election needs COMELEC approval and rules consistent with the statutory requirements. |
| A PDF's format or cryptographic protection makes it the statutory official ballot | No. Format, hashes, signatures, and labels do not alone confer that designation. |

## Questions for COMELEC Legal and policy review

The proposal should invite explicit written determinations on the following:

1. Under the selected DRE profile, what artifact is the statutory official
   ballot under RA 9369 §2(3), and how does it relate to the electronic ballot
   and recorded voting data in §2(8)?
2. What exact voter-facing operation satisfies RA 9369 §6(e) and the
   *Bagumbayan-VNP* interpretation of VVPAT? Must every voter inspect a paper
   representation, and what must it contain?
3. May the accepted signed ballot PDF be designated as the DRE machine's
   canonical electronic voting record and initial tally input, with paper
   VVPAT serving as the voter-verifiable audit record? If so, under what
   resolution, system approval, and discrepancy/recount rules?
4. Does the proposed printed output qualify as a VVPAT, an official ballot, a
   receipt, or another defined artifact? Are these terms distinct for this
   design?
5. What record controls if the PDF/canonical payload, printed VVPAT, and
   reported tally disagree? Which body conducts appreciation and adjudication?
6. What election-day counting, public reading, watcher access, custody,
   retention, and election-protest procedures must apply to each artifact?
7. Which parts require an en banc resolution, implementing rules, procurement
   clarification, technical certification, or legislation? This note does not
   assume that a policy memorandum alone is sufficient.

## Recommended language for presentation

Use the following bounded description:

> The proposed OpScan with DRE profile uses the finalized, authenticated ballot
> PDF as its **canonical electronic DRE voting record and tally input**. The
> offered system includes an A4 human-readable printed representation with the
> corresponding authenticated Truth QR for voter verification and audit. The
> proposal does not assume that the PDF replaces the statutory paper official
> ballot or VVPAT. We request COMELEC's
> determination of the applicable artifact designations, voter-verification
> procedure, counting rule, and discrepancy process under RA 9369, RA 8792,
> applicable COMELEC resolutions, and the approved procurement specifications.

## Bid and contract guidance

The bid must offer the PDF and A4 paper roles as explicit, testable parts of
one proposed DRE profile, not leave them as terminology buried in explanatory
material. State the requirements in the technical proposal, compliance matrix,
system architecture, deliverables, test plan, and draft/required contract
attachments to the extent permitted by the bidding documents.

At minimum, make the following items explicit:

1. **Electronic record and tally input.** Identify the finalized authenticated
   ballot PDF as the proposed canonical electronic DRE voting record and the
   record supplied to the approved precinct tally process. Define the cast
   event, acceptance acknowledgment, election/precinct/style context, serial,
   canonical selection data, QR relationship, signature/hash checks, retention,
   and the rule for forming the closed eligible set.
2. **A4 voter-verification paper.** Include an A4 human-readable record of the
   voter's selections, with election and precinct context and a Truth QR
   encoding the corresponding authenticated selections. Define how the voter
   privately inspects it, what happens if it is missing or disagrees with the
   confirmed selections, and how the paper is securely deposited, sealed,
   retained, and made available for audit. Ask COMELEC to designate its legal
   function and approve the final layout and workflow as part of the system.
3. **Audit procedure.** State how the printed record and QR are verified in the
   random manual audit, including any scan-and-confirm workflow, human
   observation, independent tally, discrepancy thresholds, and escalation.
   Do not represent QR-assisted tallying as already authorized if the
   applicable audit instructions prescribe a different procedure.
4. **Mismatch and exception rules.** Define the handling of a mismatch among
   voter-confirmed selections, PDF, QR, printed record, and electronic tally;
   print failure; unreadable QR; duplicate or missing serial; and voter
   objection. Preserve all evidence and specify who may resolve each case.
5. **Acceptance and certification evidence.** Tie delivery acceptance to
   witnessed end-to-end demonstrations and objective tests for voter review,
   durable acceptance, paper readability, PDF/QR/paper consistency, tally
   reproducibility, audit operation, privacy, custody, failure recovery, and
   independent verification. Identify required COMELEC/legal decisions,
   system certification, security evaluation, and procurement approvals as
   gates, not assumed outcomes.

If the proposal is evaluated, awarded, and its relevant technical commitments
are incorporated into the signed contract, that is strong evidence that the
procuring entity accepted those features as part of that contracted scope.
RA 12009 defines a bid as a signed offer/proposal and provides for post-
qualification, Notice of Award, and contract execution (especially §§5(c), 63,
and 66). An award does not automatically incorporate every statement in a
bidder's presentation: check the bidding documents' order of precedence and
ensure the requirements appear in the evaluated bid and signed contract or its
incorporated annexes. Nor does procurement acceptance waive statutes, bind a
court, substitute for a separately required certification, or authorize an
operational procedure that conflicts with election law. The contract should
identify which approvals and certifications remain conditions precedent to
operational use.

Suggested core clause for adaptation to the issued bidding documents and legal
review:

> **Proposed DRE ballot records.** The System shall create a finalized,
> authenticated ballot PDF for each confirmed voter session. For the proposed
> DRE profile, that PDF is the canonical electronic DRE voting record and
> supplies the ballot data to the precinct electronic tally process. The
> System shall also produce an A4, human-readable voter-verification paper
> record displaying the selections and a Truth QR encoding the corresponding
> election/precinct context and selections. The voter shall be provided the
> COMELEC-approved opportunity to inspect the paper record, and the paper
> record shall be secured for the approved audit procedure. The contract shall
> define the cast event, paper inspection/deposit procedure, tally rule,
> discrepancy and exception handling, custody, audit method, acceptance tests,
> and all approvals/certifications required before official operation.

This clause is a drafting starting point, not a conclusion that any specific
format or procedure already complies with law. The final response must track
the actual TOR and any bid bulletins; where they conflict with the proposed
profile, request clarification rather than claiming silent compliance.

Avoid saying that RA 8792 alone makes the PDF the official ballot, that the
2025 RMA resolution makes a VVPAT the universal primary tally source, or that
COMELEC approval is unnecessary because the file is a PDF. Those propositions
are not established by the authorities cited here.
